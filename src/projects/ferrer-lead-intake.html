What This Automation Does

Ferrer Window Co. needed a way to catch every lead from their website's contact form without anyone having to check for it manually. This n8n workflow receives each form submission, filters out obvious spam (anything missing both a phone number and an email), cleans up the data, then instantly logs the lead to a Google Sheet and posts it to the team's Slack. Both happen at once, so neither one holds up the other.

There's no live demo to click through. It runs privately inside my own n8n instance, Slack workspace, and Google Sheet, so it isn't publicly hostable. What's here instead is the real deliverable: the exported workflow (view the workflow on GitHub) and the plain-English explanation doc written for the business owner in the brief, who isn't technical and needed to understand what happens if something breaks.

How I Worked Through This

I built the workflow with n8n's Form Trigger first, even though the final version needed to run on a Webhook. I'd used Webhooks before but not Form Trigger, and starting there let me see real submission data come through without also wrestling with webhook payload structure at the same time.

For the junk-lead filter, my first instinct was to write the logic as "if phone and email are both empty, stop." I inverted it instead to "if phone or email is filled in, continue." The result is the same, but it makes the true branch the valid-data path, which is the one you actually follow when reading the workflow later.

Swapping from Form Trigger to Webhook wasn't a clean drop-in. The Webhook nests every field inside a body key alongside raw request metadata I didn't need, so every downstream reference (the filter, the field mappings) had to be updated to match. That same swap is also why the cleanup step grew: it started as a single job (defaulting an empty message field) and I rebuilt it to also strip out the metadata noise, so Sheets and Slack only ever see the five fields that actually matter.

During testing, the Google Sheets connection dropped and needed reconnecting. It was a small thing, but realistic enough that I wrote it into the troubleshooting section of the client's doc, since it's the kind of issue likely to come up again after handoff.
